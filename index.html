Order-to-Cash Analytics

              
  • Python
  • PostgreSQL
  • Power BI

              
Problem. $32.6M outstanding across 10,000 invoices, DSO drifting from ~87 to 122.6 days, and 28.8% of the debt already past 90 days. Finance could see the balance, but not where the recoverable cash was hiding.

              
What I did. Reconciled three deliberately messy CSV extracts into a trusted dataset (10 classes of data-quality issue, 22 engineered columns), wrote 15 SQL queries with CTEs and window functions, and built a 3-page Power BI dashboard on a star schema with DAX.

              

                GitHub
                
              

            

            

              
$5.2Mcash already in the bank but still chased as debt. 270 unapplied payments, 152 customers.

              
~$468Kworking capital trapped by a 9.2-day Services billing delay vs 1.9 days elsewhere.

              
$3.38Mdisputed value traced to a likely LATAM price-list root cause (20.4% dispute rate vs ~6.8%).

              02 / 04
              
Customer Shopping Behaviour

              
  • Python
  • PostgreSQL
  • Power BI

              
Problem. A retailer had revenue but no idea where it came from: which customers to retain, whether discounts bought incremental sales or just cut margin, and why so few buyers converted to subscriptions.

              
What I did. Cleaned and feature-engineered 3,900 transactions in Pandas, loaded them into PostgreSQL, answered ten business questions in SQL, segmented customers into New / Returning / Loyal, and shipped an interactive Power BI dashboard.

              

                GitHub
                
              

            

            

              
73%of customers were non-subscribers, and subscribers spent no more ($59.49 vs $59.87). The programme wasn't delivering.

              
47–50%discount rate on five products (Hat, Sneakers, Coat, Sweater, Pants). Discounting had become the default.

              
80%3,116 loyal customers carrying the business: where retention spend should actually go.

              03 / 04
              
Customer Churn Predictor

              
  • Python
  • scikit-learn
  • Streamlit

              
Problem. Retention teams reacted after cancellations, spreading discounts evenly with no way to tell who was actually at risk. Can the data a business already holds flag who is likely to leave, before they leave?

              
What I did. Built an end-to-end pipeline on 1,000 records: EDA, a compact four-feature set, five algorithms tuned with 5-fold GridSearchCV, and a linear SVM packaged with its scaler into a Streamlit app a non-technical rep can use.

              

                GitHub
                
              

            

            

              
90.5%test accuracy with a linear SVM (C = 0.01): tied for best, with the simplest decision boundary.

              
17.5 vs 30.3months of tenure, churned vs retained. Risk lives in the first two years.

              
+$13 / mohigher bills for churned customers ($75.96 vs $62.55). Bill-shock is a real trigger.

              04 / 04
              
EstateIQ — House Price Prediction

              
  • Python
  • scikit-learn
  • Streamlit

              
Problem. A buyer, a seller and a lender routinely arrive at three different prices for the same house. Give a non-expert an instant, reproducible estimate grounded in thousands of real transactions.

              
What I did. 14,619 transactions, five inputs a homeowner can answer from memory, and three models of increasing complexity (Linear Regression, tuned Decision Tree, tuned Random Forest) judged on a 2,924-property hold-out set. Deployed as EstateIQ on Streamlit.

              

                GitHub
                
              

            

            

              
R² 0.586Random Forest won on every metric: MAE ≈ 158,600 · RMSE 247,627.

              
−34%average error against a naive mean-prediction baseline of 240,169.

              
95.7%of the model's decision-making driven by living area. Square footage is the one input that must be right.
